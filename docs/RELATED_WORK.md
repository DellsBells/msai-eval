## Generalizability theory & psychometrics of ratings (Cronbach G-theory, D-studies, inter-rater reliability foundations; modern applications to NLP/LLM evaluation)

- **The Dependability of Behavioral Measurements: Theory of Generalizability for Scores and Profiles** — Lee J. Cronbach, Goldine C. Gleser, Harinder Nanda, Nageswari Rajaratnam (Wiley, New York, 1972) — https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Dependability_of_Behavioral_Measurem.html?id=dDBWAAAACAAJ
  - *why:* The founding text of generalizability theory: introduces variance decomposition of ratings across facets and the G-study/D-study distinction that MSAI's panel-as-gauge design implicitly parallels (judges = raters facet, items = objects of measurement).
- **Generalizability Theory** — Robert L. Brennan (Springer, 2001) — https://books.google.com/books/about/Generalizability_Theory.html?id=V8aaMw2pAbcC
  - *why:* The definitive modern treatment; formalizes variance-component estimation, generalizability vs. dependability coefficients, and D-study optimization of the number of raters — the psychometric analog of choosing panel size to hit a target measurement uncertainty.
- **Generalizability Theory: A Primer** — Richard J. Shavelson, Noreen M. Webb (Sage Publications, Newbury Park, 1991) — https://books.google.com/books/about/Generalizability_Theory.html?id=gzyKRZWrm9EC
  - *why:* The standard accessible primer on G-studies and decision studies; the canonical citation for the claim that measurement error has multiple simultaneously estimable sources (raters, occasions, tasks) rather than a single reliability number.
- **Survey Article: Inter-Coder Agreement for Computational Linguistics** — Ron Artstein, Massimo Poesio (Computational Linguistics 34(4):555–596, 2008) — https://aclanthology.org/J08-4004/
  - *why:* The authoritative import of the kappa/alpha agreement-coefficient tradition (Cohen, Fleiss, Krippendorff) into NLP annotation; establishes the field's default vocabulary for rater reliability that MSAI replaces with variance-based gauge metrics.
- **The "Problem" of Human Label Variation: On Ground Truth in Data, Modeling and Evaluation** — Barbara Plank (EMNLP 2022) — https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.731/
  - *why:* Position paper establishing that rater disagreement is often signal, not noise, and pervades data, modeling, and evaluation — the premise MSAI operationalizes by treating between-lineage judge disagreement as a quantified noise floor rather than something to average away.
- **Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations** — Evan Miller (arXiv:2411.00640, 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640
  - *why:* The widely cited call to treat LLM evals as statistical experiments with variance analysis and planning; MSAI extends this from confidence intervals on scores to conformity-assessment decisions with guard bands and qualification refusal.
- **Diagnosing the Reliability of LLM-as-a-Judge via Item Response Theory** — Junhyuk Choi, Sohhyung Park, Chanhee Cho, Hyeonchu Park, Bugeun Kim (ICML 2026 (arXiv:2602.00521)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00521
  - *why:* Closest psychometric neighbor: applies IRT (Graded Response Model) to diagnose LLM judge reliability via intrinsic consistency and human alignment — a latent-trait framing MSAI contrasts with its instrument-qualification framing.
- **Hidden Measurement Error in LLM Pipelines Distorts Annotation, Evaluation, and Benchmarking** — Solomon Messing (arXiv:2604.11581, 2026) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.11581
  - *why:* Decomposes LLM-pipeline uncertainty into sources (judge model choice, temperature, prompt) and distinguishes data-reducible variance from design sensitivity — the nearest precedent to MSAI's between-lineage variance component, but stops short of a gauge certificate or gating decision rule.

**Positioning:** Generalizability theory established that a rating is a sample from a universe of admissible observations, that error decomposes into estimable variance components across facets (raters, items, occasions), and that D-studies can prospectively design a measurement procedure — e.g., how many raters — to reach a target dependability coefficient (Cronbach et al. 1972; Brennan 2001; Shavelson & Webb 1991). NLP absorbed the shallower end of this tradition as chance-corrected agreement coefficients (Artstein & Poesio 2008), and recent work has begun re-importing the deeper machinery for LLM evaluation: error bars and experiment planning (Miller 2024), disagreement-as-signal (Plank 2022), IRT diagnostics of judge reliability (Choi et al. 2026), and source-wise decomposition of LLM-pipeline uncertainty (Messing 2026). All of this answers "how reliable is the instrument?" with a coefficient or an interval; none of it answers the conformity-assessment question "is this instrument qualified to gate this threshold?" MSAI supplies exactly that missing layer: it translates the variance components G-theory would estimate into a Gage R&R / GUM uncertainty budget, compares the resulting resolution to the specific decision threshold via guard-banded two-band gating, and issues four-state verdicts (including AT-EDGE) with explicit qualification refusal when between-lineage judge disagreement — the noise floor a D-study cannot design away — exceeds what the threshold demands. Where psychometrics ends with a generalizability coefficient, MSAI ends with an auditable gauge certificate: a portable artifact stating what the judge panel can and cannot resolve, in the JCGM 106 conformity-decision tradition rather than the reliability tradition. In short, MSAI treats the LLM judge panel not as a population of raters to be characterized but as a measurement system to be qualified, guard-banded, or refused.

> "offers an extensive conceptual framework and a powerful set of statistical procedures for characterizing and quantifying the fallibility of measurements" — Publisher's description of Brennan, Generalizability Theory (Springer, 2001), via Google Books
> "the literature on evaluations has largely ignored the literature from other sciences on experiment analysis and planning" — Evan Miller, Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations, arXiv:2411.00640 (2024), abstract
> "human label variation impacts all stages of the ML pipeline: data, modeling and evaluation" — Barbara Plank, The "Problem" of Human Label Variation, EMNLP 2022, abstract

## Statistical rigor for LLM evals: error bars, significance, variance decomposition

- **Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations** — Evan Miller (arXiv:2411.00640, 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640
  - *why:* The touchstone for the cluster: treats evals as experiments, gives CLT-based confidence intervals, clustered standard errors for question-group dependence, paired comparisons, and power planning. MSAI adopts its 'evals are measurements' framing but pushes past inference-about-means to instrument qualification.
- **Position: Don't Use the CLT in LLM Evals With Fewer Than a Few Hundred Datapoints** — Sam Bowyer, Laurence Aitchison, Desi R. Ivanova (ICML 2025 (Spotlight position paper); arXiv:2503.01747) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01747
  - *why:* The most prominent successor/corrective to Miller: shows CLT error bars dramatically understate uncertainty on small benchmarks and recommends frequentist/Bayesian alternatives. Directly supports MSAI's claim that naive error bars cannot certify resolution at a gated threshold.
- **Measuring All the Noises of LLM Evals** — Sida Wang (arXiv:2512.21326, 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21326
  - *why:* The closest existing variance-decomposition work: splits eval noise into prediction noise vs. data noise via the law of total variance and proposes all-pairs paired comparison; finds prediction noise typically exceeds paired data noise. MSAI's Gage R&R decomposition (repeatability/reproducibility) is the metrological analogue, extended to the judge instrument rather than the model under test.
- **Quantifying Variance in Evaluation Benchmarks** — Lovish Madaan, Aaditya K. Singh, Rylan Schaeffer, Andrew Poulton, Sanmi Koyejo, Pontus Stenetorp, Sharan Narang, Dieuwke Hupkes (arXiv:2406.10229, 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10229
  - *why:* Empirical measurement of benchmark variance (seed variance, training monotonicity) across 13 benchmarks; documents that variance is rarely quantified and tests psychometric variance-reduction methods. Establishes the 'eval noise studies' baseline MSAI builds its noise-floor argument on.
- **Quantifying Language Models' Sensitivity to Spurious Features in Prompt Design or: How I learned to start worrying about prompt formatting** — Melanie Sclar, Yejin Choi, Yulia Tsvetkov, Alane Suhr (ICLR 2024; arXiv:2310.11324) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11324
  - *why:* Canonical demonstration that prompt formatting alone induces up to 76-point accuracy spreads (FormatSpread), i.e., a huge unmodeled variance component in single-format evals. Motivates treating the judge prompt as part of the measurement system whose sensitivity must enter the uncertainty budget.
- **State of What Art? A Call for Multi-Prompt LLM Evaluation** — Moran Mizrahi, Guy Kaplan, Dan Malkin, Rotem Dror, Dafna Shahaf, Gabriel Stanovsky (TACL 2024; arXiv:2401.00595) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00595
  - *why:* Large-scale (6.5M instances, 20 models, 39 tasks) evidence that single-template evaluation is unreliable and that diverse-prompt panels are needed. Multi-prompt evaluation is the NLP analogue of MSAI's multi-appraiser Gage R&R design.
- **With Little Power Comes Great Responsibility** — Dallas Card, Peter Henderson, Urvashi Khandelwal, Robin Jia, Kyle Mahowald, Dan Jurafsky (EMNLP 2020, pp. 9263-9274) — https://aclanthology.org/2020.emnlp-main.745/
  - *why:* Pre-LLM antecedent: shows NLP experiments (GLUE, MT, human ratings) are systematically underpowered to detect the effect sizes they claim. MSAI's 'can your eval resolve the threshold it gates on?' is a conformity-assessment restatement of the power question.
- **AutoEval Done Right: Using Synthetic Data for Model Evaluation** — Pierre Boyeau, Anastasios N. Angelopoulos, Nir Yosef, Jitendra Malik, Michael I. Jordan (arXiv:2403.07008, 2024 (camera-ready 2026)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07008
  - *why:* Statistically principled use of LLM-judge labels: debiases autoevaluation against a small human-labeled set with unbiasedness guarantees (prediction-powered-inference style), boosting effective sample size ~50% with GPT-4 judges. The main rigorous treatment of judge labels as noisy measurements to be corrected, against which MSAI's qualify-the-instrument-first approach contrasts.

**Positioning:** This cluster established that LLM eval scores are noisy point estimates and imported classical inferential machinery to say so: Miller's "Adding Error Bars to Evals" supplied CLT confidence intervals, clustered standard errors, and paired-difference power planning; Bowyer et al. corrected it for the small-n regime where CLT bars dramatically understate uncertainty; Card et al. had already shown NLP comparisons are chronically underpowered; and the eval-noise studies (Madaan et al.; Wang's law-of-total-variance decomposition into prediction vs. data noise; Sclar et al. and Mizrahi et al. on prompt-induced spread) mapped where the variance actually comes from. Boyeau et al. extended rigor to LLM-as-judge specifically, debiasing judge labels against sparse human annotations with unbiasedness guarantees. What the cluster does not do is ask whether the measurement instrument is qualified to make the decision at hand: its target quantity is a model's mean score or a rank ordering, and its output is an interval or a p-value, after which the accept/reject decision is left informal. MSAI differs by applying the conformity-assessment layer literally: a Gage R&R study over a five-lineage judge panel decomposes repeatability (within-judge resampling) from reproducibility (between-lineage disagreement, taken as the irreducible noise floor rather than a nuisance to average away), rolls both into a GUM-style uncertainty budget, and issues a gauge certificate — a standing qualification artifact for the eval, not a per-comparison significance test. Guard-banding then converts the gated threshold into a two-band decision rule with four verdict states, including AT-EDGE (the guard-band zone where the panel cannot resolve the threshold) and outright qualification refusal when total gauge variation exceeds the tolerance being gated on. In short, this literature asks "is the difference significant?"; MSAI asks the prior question "can this instrument, with this uncertainty budget, legally resolve the threshold it gates on?" — and supplies the certificate and refusal semantics the error-bar literature stops short of.

> "CLT-based methods perform very poorly, usually dramatically underestimating uncertainty (i.e. producing error bars that are too small)." — Bowyer, Aitchison & Ivanova, "Position: Don't Use the CLT in LLM Evals With Fewer Than a Few Hundred Datapoints," ICML 2025, arXiv:2503.01747 (abstract)
> "Despite this widespread usage, we rarely quantify the variance in our evaluation benchmarks, which dictates whether differences in performance are meaningful." — Madaan et al., "Quantifying Variance in Evaluation Benchmarks," arXiv:2406.10229 (abstract)
> "statistical power (the probability that, given a real effect, an experiment will reject the null hypothesis) has largely been ignored by the NLP community" — Card et al., "With Little Power Comes Great Responsibility," EMNLP 2020 (abstract)

## LLM-as-judge reliability & bias: position bias, self-preference, verbosity bias, judge meta-evals (MT-Bench/Chatbot Arena, JudgeBench, G-Eval critiques)

- **Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena** — Lianmin Zheng, Wei-Lin Chiang, Ying Sheng, Siyuan Zhuang, Zhanghao Wu, Yonghao Zhuang, Zi Lin, Zhuohan Li, Dacheng Li, Eric P. Xing, Hao Zhang, Joseph E. Gonzalez, Ion Stoica (NeurIPS 2023 Datasets and Benchmarks Track) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685
  - *why:* Canonical LLM-as-judge paper; names position, verbosity, and self-enhancement bias and reports the ubiquitous >80% GPT-4/human agreement figure that MSAI reframes as an uncharacterized gauge spec rather than a capability claim.
- **Large Language Models are not Fair Evaluators** — Peiyi Wang, Lei Li, Liang Chen, Zefan Cai, Dawei Zhu, Binghuai Lin, Yunbo Cao, Qi Liu, Tianyu Liu, Zhifang Sui (arXiv 2023 (2305.17926); ACL 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926
  - *why:* Definitive position-bias demonstration — rankings hacked by reordering (Vicuna-13B beats ChatGPT on 66/80 queries under order manipulation) — plus calibration mitigations; MSAI treats such order effects as a repeatability/reproducibility component in the uncertainty budget rather than a bug to patch.
- **LLM Evaluators Recognize and Favor Their Own Generations** — Arjun Panickssery, Samuel R. Bowman, Shi Feng (arXiv 2024 (2404.13076); NeurIPS 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076
  - *why:* Establishes causal link between self-recognition and self-preference bias — the direct motivation for MSAI's multi-lineage panel design and for treating between-lineage disagreement as the noise floor.
- **Verbosity Bias in Preference Labeling by Large Language Models** — Keita Saito, Akifumi Wachi, Koki Wataoka, Youhei Akimoto (arXiv 2023 (2310.10076)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.10076
  - *why:* Quantifies verbosity bias (GPT-4 prefers longer answers more than humans) with a dedicated metric — a systematic-error term MSAI folds into the judge's bias/linearity characterization.
- **JudgeBench: A Benchmark for Evaluating LLM-based Judges** — Sijun Tan, Siyuan Zhuang, Kyle Montgomery, William Y. Tang, Alejandro Cuadron, Chenguang Wang, Raluca Ada Popa, Ion Stoica (ICLR 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12784
  - *why:* Exemplar of judge meta-evaluation with objective correctness labels; shows strong judges near-random on hard pairs — evidence that judge fitness is task-dependent, which MSAI operationalizes as per-threshold gauge qualification with an explicit refusal state.
- **G-Eval: NLG Evaluation using GPT-4 with Better Human Alignment** — Yang Liu, Dan Iter, Yichong Xu, Shuohang Wang, Ruochen Xu, Chenguang Zhu (arXiv 2023 (2303.16634); EMNLP 2023) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634
  - *why:* Widely deployed scoring-rubric judge framework whose validation is a single Spearman correlation (0.514 on summarization) and which itself flags bias toward LLM-generated text — the archetype of correlation-only validation MSAI argues cannot certify a decision threshold.
- **Justice or Prejudice? Quantifying Biases in LLM-as-a-Judge** — Jiayi Ye, Yanbo Wang, Yue Huang, Dongping Chen, Qihui Zhang, Nuno Moniz, Tian Gao, Werner Geyer, Chao Huang, Pin-Yu Chen, Nitesh V. Chawla, Xiangliang Zhang (arXiv 2024 (2410.02736); ICLR 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02736
  - *why:* CALM framework taxonomizing and automatically quantifying 12 judge bias types — the most systematic bias catalog to date; MSAI differs by rolling biases into a single combined-uncertainty figure with a pass/fail consequence rather than reporting them as a scorecard.
- **Judging the Judges: Evaluating Alignment and Vulnerabilities in LLMs-as-Judges** — Aman Singh Thakur, Kartik Choudhary, Venkat Srinik Ramayapally, Sankaran Vaidyanathan, Dieuwke Hupkes (GEM Workshop 2025 (Fourth Workshop on Generation, Evaluation and Metrics); arXiv 2406.12624) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12624
  - *why:* Judge meta-eval showing percent agreement is a misleading alignment metric (judges with high agreement can assign vastly different scores) and documenting leniency and prompt-length sensitivity — directly supports MSAI's claim that agreement statistics are not resolution statements.

**Positioning:** This cluster established that LLM judges are systematically biased measurement instruments: order of presentation flips verdicts (Wang et al.), models causally favor outputs they can recognize as their own (Panickssery et al.), verbosity is rewarded beyond human preference (Saito et al.), and CALM catalogs a dozen such distortions. It also established the field's dominant validation idiom — agreement with humans or correlation with human scores (Zheng et al.'s >80% agreement, G-Eval's Spearman 0.514) — while meta-evals like JudgeBench and Thakur et al. showed those aggregate statistics collapse on hard instances and that high percent agreement can coexist with vastly different assigned scores. What this literature does not do is close the loop from "the judge is biased" to "therefore this eval may or may not gate this threshold": biases are reported as findings or mitigated ad hoc, never composed into an uncertainty budget with a decision consequence. MSAI supplies that missing conformity-assessment layer: it treats each documented bias as a variance or systematic-error component in a GUM-style budget, runs a literal Gage R&R across a five-lineage frontier panel so self-preference is neutralized by design and between-lineage disagreement becomes the measurable noise floor, and applies guard-banded two-band gating to produce four-state verdicts including AT-EDGE. Where prior work asks "how biased is the judge?", MSAI asks the qualification question — "can this judge panel resolve the threshold it gates on?" — and, uniquely, refuses qualification when it cannot, emitting a gauge certificate as a portable artifact rather than a leaderboard number.

> "strong LLM judges like GPT-4 can match both controlled and crowdsourced human preferences well, achieving over 80% agreement, the same" — Zheng et al., Judging LLM-as-a-Judge with MT-Bench and Chatbot Arena, NeurIPS 2023 (arXiv:2306.05685 abstract)
> "The quality ranking of candidate responses can be easily hacked by simply altering their order of appearance in the context." — Wang et al., Large Language Models are not Fair Evaluators (arXiv:2305.17926 abstract)
> "judges with high percent agreement can still assign vastly different scores" — Thakur et al., Judging the Judges: Evaluating Alignment and Vulnerabilities in LLMs-as-Judges, GEM 2025 (arXiv:2406.12624 abstract)

## Science-of-evals & eval reporting standards

- **We Need a Science of Evals** — Apollo Research (Marius Hobbhahn et al.) (Apollo Research blog, January 22, 2024) — https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/we-need-a-science-of-evals
  - *why:* The manifesto that frames the field's problem statement — evals inform high-stakes safety decisions but lack the methodological maturity of engineering disciplines; MSAI answers its two core questions (right quantity, trustable results) with a literal metrology apparatus.
- **Toward an Evaluation Science for Generative AI Systems** — Laura Weidinger, Inioluwa Deborah Raji, et al. (arXiv:2503.05336, 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05336
  - *why:* Argues for importing safety-evaluation practice from mature fields (aerospace, pharma, transportation); MSAI is a concrete instantiation of exactly this program, importing AIAG MSA / GUM / JCGM 106 wholesale rather than by analogy.
- **Model evaluation for extreme risks** — Toby Shevlane, Sebastian Farquhar, Ben Garfinkel, Mary Phuong, et al. (arXiv:2305.15324, 2023) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.15324
  - *why:* Foundational case that evals should gate development/deployment decisions — precisely the threshold-gating role MSAI argues requires demonstrated measurement resolution.
- **Evaluating Frontier Models for Dangerous Capabilities** — Mary Phuong, Matthew Aitchison, Elliot Catt, et al. (Google DeepMind) (arXiv:2403.13793, 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.13793
  - *why:* The exemplar dangerous-capability eval methodology paper; its verdicts ('no evidence of strong dangerous capabilities', 'early warning signs') are exactly the kind of near-threshold statements MSAI's guard-banded four-state verdict system (including AT-EDGE) is designed to formalize.
- **STREAM (ChemBio): A Standard for Transparently Reporting Evaluations in AI Model Reports** — Tegan McCaslin, Jide Alaga, Samira Nedungadi, Seth Donoughe, Tom Reed, Rishi Bommasani, Chris Painter, Luca Righetti (arXiv:2508.09853, 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09853
  - *why:* The most developed eval reporting standard (28 criteria incl. grading/scoring and results interpretation) — but it standardizes disclosure, not measurement capability; MSAI's gauge certificate is the complementary artifact STREAM's criteria would ask to see.
- **EvalCards: A Framework for Standardized Evaluation Reporting** — Ruchira Dhar, Danae Sanchez Villegas, Antonia Karamolegkou, et al. (arXiv:2511.21695, 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.21695
  - *why:* The 'eval cards' line named in the cluster: standardized disclosure cards targeting reproducibility, accessibility, and governance — a documentation layer where MSAI supplies a qualification layer with pass/fail teeth.
- **Lessons from the Trenches on Reproducible Evaluation of Language Models** — Stella Biderman, Hailey Schoelkopf, Lintang Sutawika, Leo Gao, et al. (arXiv:2405.14782, 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14782
  - *why:* The canonical reproducibility critique from the lm-evaluation-harness team, documenting sensitivity to evaluation setup — MSAI reframes such sensitivity as quantifiable reproducibility variance in a Gage R&R decomposition.
- **Adding Error Bars to Evals: A Statistical Approach to Language Model Evaluations** — Evan Miller (Anthropic) (arXiv:2411.00640, 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00640
  - *why:* The closest statistical predecessor — brings frequentist error bars to eval scores; MSAI goes a layer further, from sampling uncertainty on a score to a full GUM uncertainty budget on the measuring instrument itself and a conformity decision rule at the gated threshold.

**Positioning:** This cluster established that evals are load-bearing governance instruments and that their methodology is not yet worthy of that load: Apollo's science-of-evals call and Weidinger et al. demand rigor imported from mature safety fields; Shevlane et al. and Phuong et al. built the dangerous-capability eval regime whose thresholds trigger real deployment decisions; Biderman et al. and Miller documented that scores are setup-sensitive and demand statistical error reporting; and STREAM and EvalCards standardized what an eval disclosure should contain. What none of these works do is qualify the measurement system itself before it is allowed to gate a threshold — they report scores (with or without error bars) but never ask whether the instrument's total measurement uncertainty is small enough to resolve the specification limit it adjudicates. MSAI supplies exactly that missing layer by applying industrial metrology literally rather than rhetorically: a Gage R&R study on an LLM judge panel, a GUM-style uncertainty budget with between-lineage disagreement as the irreducible noise floor, and a JCGM 106-style guard-banded decision rule yielding four conformity states rather than a binary pass/fail. The resulting gauge certificate is a new artifact class for this literature — where STREAM and EvalCards standardize disclosure and Miller standardizes error bars, MSAI standardizes qualification, including the refusal case: an eval whose uncertainty band swallows the threshold is declared incapable of the verdict, and the AT-EDGE state makes 'the instrument cannot resolve this' a first-class reportable outcome. In effect, MSAI converts the cluster's normative demand ('we need a science of evals') into an operational two-band gate: first the eval must pass its own gauge study, and only then may its verdict on the model be trusted.

> "Researchers and engineers face methodological issues such as the sensitivity of models to evaluation setup, difficulty of proper comparisons across methods, and the lack of reproducibility and transparency." — Biderman et al., 'Lessons from the Trenches on Reproducible Evaluation of Language Models', arXiv:2405.14782, 2024
> "We make a number of specific recommendations for running language model evaluations and reporting experiment results in a way that minimizes statistical noise and maximizes informativeness." — Evan Miller, 'Adding Error Bars to Evals', arXiv:2411.00640, 2024

## Uncertainty quantification & calibration for LLMs (conformal prediction, verbalized confidence, judge calibration) — adjacent field to differentiate from

- **Teaching Models to Express Their Uncertainty in Words** — Stephanie Lin, Jacob Hilton, Owain Evans (arXiv 2022 (TMLR)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.14334
  - *why:* Founding verbalized-confidence paper: GPT-3 taught to emit calibrated natural-language confidence ('90% confidence') without logits; the origin point of the verbalized-uncertainty line MSAI must be distinguished from.
- **Language Models (Mostly) Know What They Know** — Saurav Kadavath, Tom Conerly, Amanda Askell, Tom Henighan, et al. (arXiv 2022) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.05221
  - *why:* Canonical self-evaluation/calibration study: larger models are well-calibrated on multiple-choice/true-false in the right format and can predict P(True) of their own answers — the 'model knows what it knows' framing MSAI replaces with external instrument qualification.
- **Just Ask for Calibration: Strategies for Eliciting Calibrated Confidence Scores from Language Models Fine-Tuned with Human Feedback** — Katherine Tian, Eric Mitchell, Allan Zhou, Archit Sharma, Rafael Rafailov, Huaxiu Yao, Chelsea Finn, Christopher D. Manning (EMNLP 2023) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14975
  - *why:* Shows RLHF models' verbalized confidences beat conditional probabilities (~50% relative ECE reduction) — the strongest case that prompting can yield calibration, against which MSAI argues calibration alone does not certify threshold-resolving capability.
- **Can LLMs Express Their Uncertainty? An Empirical Evaluation of Confidence Elicitation in LLMs** — Miao Xiong, Zhiyuan Hu, Xinyang Lu, Yifei Li, Jie Fu, Junxian He, Bryan Hooi (ICLR 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.13063
  - *why:* Systematic black-box confidence-elicitation benchmark documenting persistent verbalized overconfidence — motivates MSAI's move from self-reported confidence to externally measured repeatability/reproducibility.
- **Semantic Uncertainty: Linguistic Invariances for Uncertainty Estimation in Natural Language Generation** — Lorenz Kuhn, Yarin Gal, Sebastian Farquhar (ICLR 2023 (Spotlight)) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.09664
  - *why:* Semantic entropy — the leading sampling-based UQ measure for generation; represents the within-model dispersion school that MSAI contrasts with between-lineage disagreement as the noise floor.
- **Conformal Language Modeling** — Victor Quach, Adam Fisch, Tal Schuster, Adam Yala, Jae Ho Sohn, Tommi S. Jaakkola, Regina Barzilay (ICLR 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.10193
  - *why:* Canonical conformal-prediction-for-LLM-outputs paper: distribution-free coverage guarantees via calibrated prediction sets — the per-item statistical-guarantee paradigm MSAI's system-level gauge certificate differs from.
- **Black-box Uncertainty Quantification Method for LLM-as-a-Judge** — Nico Wagner, Michael Desmond, Rahul Nair, Zahra Ashktorab, Elizabeth M. Daly, Qian Pan, Martín Santillán Cooper, James M. Johnson, Werner Geyer (arXiv 2024) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.11594
  - *why:* Closest prior art on judge-specific UQ: derives high/low-uncertainty labels for individual judge assessments from token-probability confusion matrices — flags unreliable verdicts but never asks whether the judge system can resolve the gating threshold.
- **Analyzing Uncertainty of LLM-as-a-Judge: Interval Evaluations with Conformal Prediction** — Huanxin Sheng, Xinyi Liu, Hangfeng He, Jieyu Zhao, Jian Kang (EMNLP 2025) — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18658
  - *why:* Most directly adjacent work: conformal prediction intervals (with ordinal boundary adjustment) around LLM-judge scores from a single run — per-item intervals with exchangeability guarantees, versus MSAI's GUM budget, guard-banded four-state verdicts, and qualification refusal.

**Positioning:** This cluster established that LLM uncertainty can be estimated and, to a degree, calibrated: verbalized confidence (Lin; Tian) and self-evaluation (Kadavath) attach a probability to each answer, semantic entropy (Kuhn) measures within-model dispersion over resampled generations, and conformal methods (Quach; Sheng) wrap individual outputs or judge scores in distribution-free prediction sets and intervals — with judge-specific work (Wagner; Sheng) flagging unreliable verdicts item by item. The shared paradigm is per-prediction uncertainty reporting: the deliverable is a confidence score, entropy value, or coverage-guaranteed interval attached to each output, and success is measured by calibration error or empirical coverage. MSAI inverts the object of study: it characterizes the judge panel itself as a measurement system, using Gage R&R variance decomposition and a GUM-style uncertainty budget to produce a gauge certificate — a qualification artifact for the instrument, not an interval for an item. It then adds the layer this literature lacks entirely: a JCGM 106-style conformity-decision rule with guard-banded acceptance zones, yielding four-state verdicts (including AT-EDGE, where the measured value sits inside the guard band and no pass/fail claim is licensed) under a two-band gate. Crucially, MSAI admits qualification refusal — if between-lineage disagreement, taken as the irreducible noise floor rather than within-model resampling dispersion, exceeds what the gating threshold requires, the eval is declared unable to resolve that threshold and is rejected outright, an outcome no calibration or conformal framework produces. A perfectly calibrated or coverage-valid judge can still fail MSAI qualification: calibration says the reported uncertainty is honest, while the gauge certificate says whether the uncertainty is small enough to gate on.
